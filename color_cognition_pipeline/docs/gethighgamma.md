We then extracted ECoG signals in the broadband gamma range, i.e., signals widely recognized to represent local cortical population activity. To do this, we first removed signal drifts (2 Hz high-pass, Butterworth, order 4), rereferenced the signals with a common-average-reference montage, band-pass filtered the results (110–140 Hz, Butterworth, order 4), applied the Hilbert transform, extracted the analytic amplitude, down-sampled (to 500 Hz) and square-root transformed the results, and finally normalized them by z-scoring with respect to all baseline periods (the 200 ms immediately preceding stimulus presentation). For each location, we then determined whether its average broadband gamma responses between 100 and 500 ms after stimulus presentation were selective to faces or colors by using unpaired/paired Wilcoxon rank-sum tests (faces vs. objects/colored vs. grayscale contrasts, respectively, P < 0.05 Bonferroni-corrected by the number of electrodes).
